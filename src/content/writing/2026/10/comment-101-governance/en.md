A comment section determines both which voices are seen and which content remains. Ranking and moderation are connected: moderation status determines eligibility to appear, while ranking allocates limited attention. The restaurant place listing (point of interest, or POI) from the series shows how comments enter ranking and review, then get withdrawn downstream after corrections or staleness.

## 1. Comment presentation, ranking, and replies

Comment ranking should keep two axes separate. **Visibility** asks whether a comment can be seen; **usefulness** asks whether it helps the reader complete a task. A single quality score can confuse “ranked near the top” with “useful information.” Measure the axes separately, then check whether highly useful comments receive a fair chance to be seen.

|  | High usefulness | Low usefulness |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **High visibility** | Specific, timely, verifiable information reaches the right reader | Generic praise, conflict, or repetition takes the top spots and may crowd out useful information |
| **Low visibility** | Specific long-tail experience, small-language reviews, or later corrections are buried | Spam, stale information, and empty content sink or are moderated |

Measure visibility through exposure coverage, position distribution, language and regional reach, and exploration opportunities for new comments. Measure usefulness through specificity, freshness, task resolution, provenance, and retrieval reuse. Ranking should test whether useful comments get more effective exposure. If the two are unrelated, the ranking signals have not captured task value. Safety, privacy, and permissions remain eligibility conditions; usefulness does not offset a violation.

Platform features below are described from public materials. Product recommendations are my own proposals, not claims about internal ranking rules.

Existing products show several forms. TikTok introduced Q&A in 2021: creators can mark comments as questions, answer in text or video, and link a video response back to the original question. In 2026, TikTok announced comment polls, voice comments, and photo carousels. [TikTok Q&A](https://newsroom.tiktok.com/qa-feature-rolls-out-to-all-creators?lang=en-GB); [TikTok comment updates](https://newsroom.tiktok.com/say-more-in-the-comments?lang=en). YouTube Studio lets creators heart and pin comments, filter by questions or response status, and use AI-generated comment topics on eligible videos. [YouTube Studio comment tools and topics](https://support.google.com/youtubecreatorstudio/answer/9482367?co=GENIE.Platform%3DDesktop). Comments can therefore be interaction, a queue of questions, an idea pool for the next piece of content, or a topic index.

The effect of AI summaries on comment supply is covered in the series’ third article. Here, the focus is how ranking presents a summary alongside its sources, freshness, and differences of opinion.

Some features change what a “top comment” means. Reddit lets moderators recommend a community’s default comment sort: Q&A for question-and-answer communities, or options such as Top and Live for others. Communities can also hide scores for a period so readers see the content before the vote count. These settings let a community’s task shape ranking, but a default is not a factual judgment and does not remove first-mover advantage. Public descriptions of X show another approach: the system retrieves reply candidates, uses reader and comment features to predict likes, replies, reposts, and negative feedback, then rebuilds the conversation. It can flatten a conversation tree or select only some branches, while higher-risk replies enter a health-ranking tier. Ranking is more than calculating one score; it includes candidate selection, thread structure, and governance tiers. [Reddit community comment settings](https://support.reddithelp.com/hc/en-us/articles/15484546290068-Community-settings)

Common list designs sort by time or popularity, or place author replies and pinned comments first. Chronological order is good for catching up; popularity surfaces discussions readers have recognized. Raw like counts favor comments that appeared early or received more exposure. An author reply can add context to the main content, but it is not automatically what readers care about most. If “funniest comment” means “most liked,” the feature can quickly become a contest in follower count.

A product can let users switch between `Newest` and `Popular`, with task-specific views such as `Answered`, `Author replies`, or `Live`. A tutorial reader may want extra steps and resolved questions. For a short drama or video, live comments and a creator pin may add to the experience. In a product review, model numbers, conditions of use, and counterexamples may be more useful than a joke. If constructive tone, curiosity, or personal experience enters ranking, validate it by content task. An experiment with 460 US English-speaking readers found that ranking comments for constructiveness and curiosity improved perceived respect, credibility, and interest on a political article. On a dining article, personal stories and constructive comments increased perceived informativeness. Effects varied by article type, so they should not become one fixed site-wide weight. [Experiment on ranking for constructiveness and curiosity](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.05429)

Commercial content can also turn comments into a conversion path. TikTok Shop lets eligible creators add a product link when answering a product question. That can shorten the path from question to product page, while also making the comment area a sales surface. If a product uses a similar mechanic, disclose the product and commercial relationship, limit repetitive pitches in a thread, and record “problem solved” separately from “purchase clicked.” A product recommendation should not pass itself off as a community highlight just because it converts.

For a product built around practical experience, I would start with three features: question labels and resolved status, an inbox for unanswered questions to authors, and comment-topic groupings that link back to the source comments. They improve answer discovery and comment handling, and produce interpretable signals for search and recommendations. Polls and multimodal comments fit some content types better than others. Adding them everywhere just to raise comment volume may produce low-information responses and increase moderation work. A useful feature leaves a clearer answer, relationship, or content trail after the user takes an extra step.

Reply depth should follow the product task; one UI rule should not dictate every data model. For articles, products, or places where the main purpose is to add evidence and answer questions quickly, the default interface can limit replies to one level beneath a root comment and use `reply_to_comment_id` to identify the specific comment being answered. This is easier to scan and reduces indentation and notification complexity. Public discussions in the style of Reddit need deeper nesting because rebuttals, clarifications, and lines of reasoning are part of the content. A database can retain `parent_id`, `root_id`, and `depth`, while each product configures its allowed reply depth, default expansion, folding, and context links. Flattening the interface should not erase the actual reply relationship.

If a product wants a “top comment” mechanic, define clear candidate types such as creator picks, community favorites, answered questions, and saved comments, each with its own selection rule. Selection needs abuse controls, a cold-start window, downranking after reversals or reports, and manual correction. Record the source of every manual boost so operations can be analyzed separately from organic interaction.

The ranking path can be summarized as eligibility filtering, task organization, candidate ranking, exposure correction, and list presentation. Keep pins, creator picks, commercial placements, and organic popularity separately labeled. Safety and permissions are hard gates; a low score cannot offset a violation. Correct popular comments for position and time, and give new comments an opportunity to be evaluated.

## 2. Permissions, corrections, and user control

It is not enough to record deletion, editing, and appeal events in a data stream. Users also need to know what they can control. Make status understandable: people should be able to distinguish “under review,” “temporarily hidden,” and “deleted,” and see the reason, appeal path, and outcome where appropriate. Section 6 covers how indexes, notification previews, and recommendation features follow a state change.

Content authors and comment authors have different controls. Within the permissions a platform grants, an author may close comments, limit who can comment, pin or hide comments on their content. A commenter may edit or delete their own comment, mute reply notifications, or report content. The product should distinguish what “author deletion” means: hiding a comment from one’s own comment area, deleting one’s own comment, or reporting another comment to the platform. Creators should not be allowed to rewrite someone else’s comment, erase moderation evidence, or bypass appeals. A negative review is not a violation merely because its view is unfavorable. China’s Administrative Provisions on Internet Comment Services allow public-account operators, under service agreements, to request reporting, hiding, or removal of illegal or harmful comments and to close comment areas; platforms set permissions based on account type and credit assessment. This does not authorize an author to delete any comment at will. [Administrative Provisions on Internet Comment Services, Articles 6 and 10 to 13](https://www.cac.gov.cn/2022-11/16/c_1670253725725039.htm)

If a user blocks someone, that person’s content should not reappear through top comments, search summaries, or related recommendations. A block should not replace the platform’s own violation judgment. Notifications are another route content can escape through: replies, likes, or an author’s acceptance can trigger a notification, while bulk likes and low-value activity should be bundled, rate-limited, or made optional. When a comment is deleted or a decision is reversed, remove its text preview from associated notifications so it does not continue circulating after the comment disappears.

A comment may identify an error in the main content, add a condition of use, or report that a product batch, store hours, or software version has changed. Do not dismiss that negative information as low quality or remove it from organic ranking because it hurts conversion. Structured labels such as “correction,” “out of date,” and “resolved” let an author or merchant respond to a specific comment and change. Have operations staff or trusted contributors review high-impact factual claims; leave ordinary opinions as opinions rather than turning them into platform conclusions. Show when time-sensitive information was posted and allow an update when conditions change.

There is also a limit to platform power. A platform sets the rules, moderates content, and handles disputes about its decisions. A merchant complaint does not automatically make a comment a violation; a reviewer’s claim of a real purchase does not exempt the comment from verification either. E-commerce law in China states the boundary more directly: Article 39 of the E-Commerce Law requires platforms to provide a way to review products and services and prohibits deleting consumer reviews of goods or services sold on the platform. My normative view is that specific disputes still need to be handled under applicable law and rules; making a page look better is not a reason to remove a review. [E-Commerce Law of the People’s Republic of China, Article 39](https://www.samr.gov.cn/zfjcj/tzgg/art/2023/art_d337c3291e8b40459ca03dea54395856.html)

The state machine should distinguish removal for a violation, concealment for privacy or safety, withdrawal by the author, and a merchant dispute awaiting review. A merchant complaint should create an evidence-backed review case, not hide the comment by itself. Record the reason, evidence, operator, notification, and appeal outcome. Meituan’s public rules offer an example: merchant appeals require evidence, and the platform uses system, human, or public review depending on the dispute. It also says that, in principle, it respects a consumer’s account of personal experience. [Meituan review-dispute process](https://rules-center.meituan.com/m/detail/guize/255?activeRule=1); [Meituan merchant review-appeal rules](https://rules-center.meituan.com/m/detail/guize/901)

Alongside the rate of fake reviews that pass, sample the rate at which genuine negative reviews are wrongly removed, the share of merchant complaints upheld as actual violations, reinstatement after appeal, and differences in handling by star rating or sentiment. Otherwise, a system can optimize “fewer merchant complaints” into “fewer visible critical reviews,” producing a tidier page and less reliable information.

**Governance flow: from eligibility to risk handling.**

Ranking determines how comments compete for attention. Moderation and abuse controls determine whether content can be published, remain visible, or be appealed. They share comment objects and state records but answer different questions.

## 3. Moderation is more than a boolean

Comments carry risk and context. “Have you tried this?” may be a normal follow-up under a product review, or part of a provocation in a personal dispute. A simple `approved: true/false` field cannot represent queues, secondary review, appeals, or which version of a policy informed a decision.

A practical publishing flow begins by storing a submission as `pending`, with the original text and submission metadata. Automated moderation handles rule matches, spam, duplicate flooding, risk categories, and confidence. It rejects or isolates clearly high-risk content; lower-risk content can proceed toward publication when policy allows. Uncertain cases, high-impact categories, or conflicts between models go to a human. The first human review classifies the case and records evidence. Secondary review handles disputes, high-risk cases, and appeals, and samples initial decisions. “First review” and “secondary review” are human workflows; automated moderation supports decisions and automatic blocking; AI sampling is quality control. They are not three serial gates every comment must pass.

After a decision, a comment can become `visible`. Other states might include `pending_review`, `held`, `rejected`, `removed`, and `appealed`. Show users the necessary outcome and an appeal path. Both pre-publication review and post-publication governance matter: harmful content can evade detection, and risk can change as a conversation develops. If a published comment is reported or new evidence appears, the case should be reopened rather than forcing the platform to delete the entire discussion.

AI sampling can uncover two kinds of error: risky comments that were allowed through, and normal expression that was blocked. Do not sample uniformly from approved comments alone; that cannot reveal whether human rejections are too strict. Stratify by decision, risk type, model version, reviewer, and traffic volume, then feed review findings back into rules and models. Moderation records should retain at least the input-object version, model and rule versions, confidence, match reasons, human action, operator, timestamp, and appeal outcome. An edited comment should be reviewed again. If it already has replies, the parent’s state should inform how the thread is shown.

The primary moderation metrics are not approval rate. Track false-negative and false-positive rates, reversals in secondary review, appeal overturns, processing time, and exposure time for risky content. Also check **whether users whose comments are blocked, collapsed, or reinstated after appeal return to contribute**. By new versus returning users, comment task, rule category, and review outcome, observe the probability of a next useful contribution and completion of a first contribution within 7 or 30 days. This metric alone cannot prove moderation caused a user to leave, but it can alert the team that a threshold may be blocking normal newcomers along with harmful content. Stratified sampling, appeal review, and controlled policy experiments help evaluate safety gains against supply losses. Decisions must follow applicable law and platform rules; this is a product and data architecture proposal, not a description of any platform’s internal process.

Governance should also consider whether users accept the treatment. For relational harm such as insults and threats, removal or limiting distribution often matches user expectations. For misinformation, users may prefer a warning, added context, or rebuttal over unexplained deletion. A factorial survey of a representative Austrian sample (572 participants) found a preference for removing insulting content and for interactive interventions on misinformation. Overall, participants trusted human moderators more than AI moderators. These findings come from one country and experimental setting; they do not establish a universal community policy. [Wiesner: A user-centered evaluation of content moderation strategies](https://doi.org/10.1080/1369118X.2025.2557548)

Product actions can vary by risk: remove, collapse, limit distribution, add a warning label or guideline reminder, provide a source, or allow a rebuttal. Gray-area content needs particular care. An experiment on moderation visibility found that trust differed more between AI and human moderation in ambiguous harassment cases than in clear-cut cases. [Study of AI moderation visibility, accountability, and trust](https://doi.org/10.1177/20539517221115666) Human review therefore offers more than a route to accuracy; it gives users an accountable process where they can appeal and ask for a reason. Add acceptance surveys by violation type, disagreement rates between AI and human reviewers, secondary human-review rates for ambiguous cases, and appeal reversals by action.

Moderation delays can also affect supply. A conversation may cool while a comment waits; the questioner may leave, and the contributor may not return. Track P50/P90/P99 time from submission to first visibility, the share of comments eventually published after review, questioners who leave before release, time hidden during appeal, and contributor return within 7 or 30 days after review. Segment by content risk, contributor tenure, and outcome to estimate the relationship between delay and later contribution. Then test whether changes to routing thresholds or queues increase supply while keeping false negatives and false positives in check. A fast lane for trusted contributors can be tested, with sampling, limited permissions, and a way to revoke access.

## 4. Abuse controls must detect both content risk and manipulation

Prohibited-word review is only the entry point. Comment safety has two separate questions: can this content be published, and is this interaction trustworthy? The first covers illegal or prohibited information, abuse, threats, privacy leaks, scams, and off-platform solicitation. The second covers mass account creation or trading, automated likes and reviews, copy-paste spam, coordinated review manipulation, fake endorsements, and ranking abuse. Both can appear in one comment, but they should not collapse into a single “low-quality” score. A normal user can write a low-quality comment without being part of an abuse operation; an abusive account can post text that is perfectly compliant on its face.

Abuse groups often want to manipulate visible outcomes, not merely get one comment past review: push a comment to the top, manufacture praise for a product, suppress criticism, amplify a trending topic, or take a user to an off-platform transaction. Detection therefore needs to look beyond individual text to accounts, devices or sessions, time series, similarity clusters, interaction networks, and campaign waves. Signals might include repeated posts in a short period, abnormal interaction rhythms, fixed mutual-like groups, clusters of new accounts responding to the same content, similar copy spreading across items, rapid edits or deletions after exposure, and a mismatch between exposure and interaction sources. One signal should usually raise a risk score, not trigger a verdict. Enforcement should combine signals, account history, context, and human review so that a natural surge of discussion around a major event is not mislabeled as a coordinated attack.

A prohibited-word list is useful for high-confidence, low-latency rules. It cannot make semantic judgments on its own. Manage rules by policy category, context, and version. Distinguish terms that can be blocked directly, terms that need context, and permitted quotation or educational use. Normalization, OCR, and speech recognition can help detect homographs, obfuscated characters, wordplay, text in images, and spoken content. A match alone should not permanently ban an account. Preserve the original text, matched span, rule version, and action rationale. Common false positives should feed an allowlist or rule revision process; every allowlist entry needs a scope, owner, and expiry.

A risk-tiered system can act differently at each level: reject or remove confirmed violations; hold high-risk but uncertain cases for human review; temporarily exclude suspicious engagement from popularity, recommendation, and search features while an account cluster is checked; and rate-limit or verify low-risk new accounts instead of permanently banning them. For coordinated manipulation, enforcement should link affected comments, accounts, and abnormal interaction edges, then roll back contaminated counts and ranking features. User reports are valuable leads, not verdicts. Coordinated reporting can itself be used to silence normal expression.

Keep an auditable evidence chain: content version, behavior window, model and rule versions, linked account cluster, confidence range, action, human review, and appeal outcome. Store account risk separately from the comment’s moderation decision, with retention periods and access controls. Device and relationship signals should be necessary and privacy-bounded; a temporary risk label should not become a permanent user profile. China’s Provisions on the Governance of the Online Information Content Ecosystem require platforms to establish systems for comment review, real-time inspection, incident response, and handling information about black-market chains. Public notices from Cyberspace Administration authorities have also identified fake reviews and likes, bulk account manipulation, and vote or ranking fraud as astroturfing problems. These rules do not reveal any platform’s internal model or thresholds. [Provisions on the Governance of the Online Information Content Ecosystem](https://www.cac.gov.cn/2019-12/20/c_1578375159509309.htm?eqid=cf2ac2ed00075698000000056440e8f4); [Cyberspace Administration notice on combating online astroturfing](https://www.cac.gov.cn/2025-1/3/c_1737254417150320.htm)

Do not measure abuse control by the number of blocks alone. Track violations that pass, legitimate content incorrectly blocked, the share of manipulated engagement, ranking changes after invalid activity is removed, time from first signal to containment, recurrence, appeal reversals, and moderation cost. Break these down by risk type, account tenure, content category, and entry point to make sure the model is not suppressing newcomers, minority expression, or active communities wholesale. Moderation models assess content risk; anti-abuse models assess whether behavior is trustworthy; ranking consumes signals after governance. They should work through versioned result interfaces rather than overwrite one another’s source records.

### 4.1 AI-written comments are not the same as fake reviews

“Was this written by AI?” does not answer whether a comment is trustworthy. Separate at least four dimensions: **authorship method** (human-written, AI-assisted, AI-written, or unknown); **experience basis** (first-hand experience and any order or visit evidence); **factual accuracy** (whether claims can be checked and are current); and **publishing intent** (sharing an experience, a disclosed commercial incentive, review manipulation, or a scam). AI can help a user phrase a real experience; a comment from a human account can still describe a visit that never happened. A verified purchase or visit strengthens the evidence that a transaction or visit occurred, not that the opinion is objective.

AI-generated replies from the platform should be labeled as system content, never presented as user comments, and never counted as human community supply through their likes or replies. If AI-assisted reviews are allowed, the interface can let users disclose or mark the assistance. If a category requires first-hand experience, verify the experience basis and any incentive relationship. AI-text detection should remain a low-confidence supporting signal: adversarial rewrites can evade it, and language or writing-style differences can trigger false positives. One evaluation observed substantially lower detector recall on some datasets; a separate study of English writing by non-native speakers reported high false-positive rates. Those results cannot be converted directly into error rates for Chinese comments. [Evaluation of AI text detectors](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.05139); [Study of GPT detectors and bias against non-native English writers](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10382961/)

Multimodal comments can preserve provenance credentials. Standards such as C2PA record and validate signed provenance claims and edit history, but a valid credential does not make its claims true. OpenAI’s public provenance guidance also notes that metadata can be stripped and that “not detected” does not mean “definitely not AI-generated.” Valid credentials can strengthen provenance; missing credentials do not prove human authorship. Combine text, account history, experience evidence, similarity clusters, and interaction networks. Preserve the confidence and evidence behind a detection decision, with human review and an appeal path. [C2PA Content Credentials specification](https://spec.c2pa.org/specifications/specifications/2.2/specs/C2PA_Specification); [OpenAI guidance on content provenance](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/content-provenance)

Track AI-assistance disclosure, experience-evidence coverage, sampled fake experiences, coordinated review manipulation, false-positive and false-negative rates by authorship method, human overrides, and appeal reversals separately. Authenticity is not an `is_ai` boolean and should not be decided by one model score.

### 4.2 Generated content and GEO: how comment evidence gets polluted

This section focuses on one path: generated content and Generative Engine Optimization (GEO) campaigns can fabricate experiences or a false consensus, undermining the credibility of comments as decision evidence. The effect of summaries on supply appears in the third article; it is a different risk and calls for different metrics.

The misleading mechanism is similar for virtual and physical consumption, but the harm lands differently. Games, subscriptions, and digital courses are vulnerable to fabrications about whether a product exists, what it does, or whether it will continue to be delivered. For restaurants, hotels, and local services, false word of mouth can distort expectations about quality, hygiene, location, and service, leading to a bad purchase, a wasted trip, or more serious real-world consequences. Require different evidence by context. An order or visit credential shows that a transaction or visit occurred; it does not prove a review is objective. AI-text detection is also supporting evidence, not a verdict.

Treat provenance as a distinct data dimension: account and author relationships, transaction or visit evidence, incentive disclosures, media credentials, moderation state, and evidence level should remain separate. AI summaries and search answers should link to source comments and show their date and coverage. When a source is deleted, overturned, or stale, trigger summary recomputation, index removal, and updates to derived features. Measure the share of fake content reaching ranking or answers, important negative facts omitted from summaries, time to withdraw a source, and unusual disappearance of genuine negative reviews, rather than whether a page looks tidier.

## 5. The main content and comment area should not share one prohibited-word list

Platform rules and laws below reflect publicly available versions as of October 2026. Apply current local law and platform rules to specific cases; the product examples describe governance design, not legal advice.

Moderation should share a common baseline without treating article text, comments, direct messages, live danmaku, and product Q&A as one surface. They differ in length, participant relationships, distribution, and business risk. The same phrase might appear in an article warning about fraud, or in a comment saying, “Add me on WeChat; we’ll handle your refund privately.” A literal keyword match could block the first and miss the second when it uses wordplay, an image, speech, or contextual cues. A more useful unit is “phrase + surface + parent or reply context + account behavior + policy version.”

Illegal content still has to be handled under the common baseline. Individual surfaces can use different rules and actions.

Anti-fraud controls need their own comment-safety path. Fake customer service, refund or compensation scams, investment and loan promises, requests for verification codes or bank details, unfamiliar links, and QR codes pose a risk beyond the text itself: will a user leave the platform and hand over money or personal information? The system should consider user reports, account history, bursts of outreach, comment and reply chains, link and QR detection, and related spread after a report. When risk is confirmed, it can first block the link or contact details, hide the comment, limit further replies, and, where useful, direct users to official in-platform service or reporting channels. Uncertain cases should go to review; do not ban every sentence that mentions a refund, support, or contact details. Track potential-victim exposure, time from detection to containment, false positives, and appeal reversals alongside block counts.

Moderation also needs to protect information users do not want made public: phone numbers, home addresses, order numbers, payment records, precise location, and identifying details about minors. Scan beyond the text. Images, subtitles, and speech transcripts can expose the same information. Before publication, the product can warn users, mask identifiable fields by default, or send them for review. Moderation samples, indexes, and training features should follow data minimization, access controls, and retention limits; apply the relevant deletion policy to derived copies when a user withdraws a comment. For minors, take extra care with identity exposure, contact from strangers, and commercial solicitation. More interaction is not automatically a successful outcome.

Record `surface`, `policy_scope`, matched span, context object, risk type, rule and model versions, confidence, action, and appeal outcome explicitly. A decision about off-platform solicitation in a comment should not contaminate the article’s moderation label. Search, recommendation, and anti-abuse systems need to distinguish a policy risk from low-quality text or a trustworthy account of an experience. Shared policies define what should not appear; surface-specific strategies define how to judge and handle it. Maintain the two layers separately.

## 6. State propagation, versions, and withdrawal

Lifecycle operations are more than a `DELETE` statement. When an author edits a comment, review the new version and retain whatever version history is needed for appeals and audit. When a user deletes a comment, remove the public text under the retention policy and propagate deletion or withdrawal events to interaction links, counters, caches, search indexes, and training data. Whether training samples must be physically erased immediately depends on privacy, legal, and retention requirements. At the product level, deleted content should no longer appear as a visible answer or positive recommendation signal. When the parent content is removed, comments should follow its access permissions and stop being retrieved. If a parent comment disappears, the product can show a “comment unavailable” placeholder or fold the branch, so child replies do not become context-free fragments.

State changes affect comments and downstream indexes. Once a correction is confirmed, search and AI answers should favor the updated fact while preserving any necessary correction history. An edited, hidden, or withdrawn comment’s old version should not remain the current answer. E-commerce reviews also need to distinguish product issues from shipping, after-sales service, and individual conditions of use; a merchant reply does not by itself mean the problem was resolved. Track correction confirmation, time to correction, exposure of stale information, and unusual changes in the visibility of negative experiences.

Return to the restaurant POI review from the opening. The author later finds that “about a 20-minute wait on weekends” is out of date and replaces it with the wait from a recent visit. The system retains the version and experience date, reviews the update, and emits index and feature invalidation or recomputation events. The place page, search candidates, summary cache, and recommendation features must stop using the old time condition. If the comment is overturned or deleted, withdraw training samples according to their use and retention policy. Only then does the correction change what future readers see, rather than merely changing the comment detail page.

These permissions belong in the moderation state machine and event contract. Record the operator, object version, reason, and scope. Define who can restore content, who can appeal, whether a comment stays hidden during appeal, and how a parent-state change affects replies. The boundary of the comment experience is not just the moment something is posted; it also includes whether a user can later edit, withdraw, correct, and stop receiving unwanted contact.
